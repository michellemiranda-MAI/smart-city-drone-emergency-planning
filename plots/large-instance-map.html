Details to point out in the report

  • Both unlock kinds: a delivery opens petrochem, an inspection opens part_alta.
  • Chained goals: inspecting amfiteatre is needed before kit1 can be delivered, and delivering comm1 before petrochem can be inspected.
  • bonavista is a charger and a delivery site at once. A type could not express that; predicates can.
  • One cost-3 edge (bonavista to campus) adds a third row to the battery table: 6 + 5 + 4 = 15 facts.

Sequences that fail

(fly droneA bonavista petrochem b4 b1 b3) before comm1 is delivered: fly requires (accessible petrochem), which only deliver-supply of comm1 adds.

(fly droneB rambla part_alta b6 b2 b4) before amfiteatre is inspected: (accessible part_alta) is false, so the flight is not applicable.

Where does the 3-drone advantage come from?

Three drones can beat one in two ways: they bring three batteries and three cargo slots, and they can start closer to their goals. A third variant separates the two: three drones that all start at the port depot, on the same map with the same goals.

Optimal costs from A* + LM-cut. *Model B, clustered: A* hit the 5-minute limit after proving the optimum is at least 31; the best plan found (LAMA) costs 33.

Most of the saving comes from extra batteries and cargo slots, not from where the drones start. One drone carries one supply at a time and runs out of battery, so it keeps returning to depots and chargers. Model B makes this worse because each lost level costs a separate recharge action. That is why the gap grows from 21% to 38%. Start positions matter less on this map because the hub at rambla and the river crossing put every region within a few levels of both depots.
